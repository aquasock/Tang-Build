The HDMI defect is the flow, not the design: same RTL, Gowin builds it working and the
open flow does not

2026-10-10.  This is the experiment the afternoon was building towards, and it
is controlled: **one design, two flows**.  No source, chipdb or RTL was changed
for it.

## The confound it removes

Earlier the honest comparison was Phosphor working on HDMI and the desktop core
not, on the same board, cable, capture card and PC.  That reads as "the toolchain
is the problem", but Phosphor differs from `desktop.bin` in **two** ways at once
-- the flow *and* the design -- so it isolated nothing.  What was needed was the
same RTL through both flows.

## The two artifacts

    flow    artifact                                     stamp        built
    open    build/clock-monitor/desktop.90d3fa3a84fa.bin 90d3fa3a84fa seed 17 P&R, setup 0 hold 0
    Gowin   /tmp/vendor-monitor/place2/desktop.bin       95e9b3e1cb88 tools/build_desktop_core.sh 2, timing PASS

Both are the **current design including the clock monitor**, from the same
sources; the Gowin one was built earlier the same day.  Each was `tangput` to the
card and loaded with `tangload`, and the HDMI was captured from the
MACROSILICON card with 120 frames of 1280x720 YUYV.

## The measurement, by chroma

Chroma is the discriminator (evidence/hdmi-instrument-control.txt): a real
picture varies in it, and the card's own monochrome idle field and overlay both
sit at exactly 128.

    open seed 17     120 frames   max chroma distinct =  1   flat 59 + monochrome overlay 61
    Gowin same RTL   120 frames   max chroma distinct = 24   COLOUR 119 + 1 transition
    Phosphor (Gowin)  60 frames   max chroma distinct = 22   COLOUR 59  + 1 transition

The Gowin build of this design delivers a colour picture on HDMI and holds it for
the whole capture.  The open build of the same design delivers no colour in any
frame.  Phosphor, also Gowin-built, behaves like the Gowin desktop build.

## What it establishes

**The flow is the variable.**  With the design held constant, the vendor flow
produces working HDMI and the open flow produces none.  That confirms the
mechanism the project had already located rather than replacing it: the vendor's
place-and-route selects the HCLK network's **GAMMA** input at the divider for the
371.25 MHz TMDS bit clock, while nextpnr selects **ALPHA/BETA**, fed by `L2HCLK`,
the **fabric entry**, because the chipdb's network input is unconnected
(evidence/hclk-route-gap.txt).  In the open build `hclk5` fails dedicated routing
at all four of its sinks with 2.759 ns of skew on a 2.6936 ns bit period.

**And it answers the question that prompted it**: an open-built Phosphor would
very likely lose HDMI the same way.  The mechanism is the flow's treatment of a
371.25 MHz clock on its way to the serialisers, and it would apply to any design
that flow places -- Phosphor only works today because Gowin placed it.

## What follows

The project's remaining gap is now precise and correctly aimed: **the open flow
cannot route the TMDS bit clock to the serialisers**, and the fix is the chipdb's
clock network -- the modelling work of entries 64 to 66 -- and not the design,
the seating, the cable, the monitor, the firmware or the capture path, each of
which is now excluded by experiment.

## Limits

One Gowin build against one open build of the same RTL.  "The flow" is isolated
as a whole: the Gowin build differs in **synthesis and place-and-route** both, so
this does not say which of the two steps decides it -- though the routing evidence
points at place-and-route, since the bit clock's bind is a placement outcome.  The
captures are two seconds each, and the frames are classified by chroma rather than
read as pictures; the PNGs at `/tmp/C_gowin-same-RTL-frame30.png` and
`/tmp/B_desktop-frame0.png` are the visual check.
