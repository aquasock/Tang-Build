# clock-smoke as a three-PLL instrument, and the decoder correction it forced

# Design:   fpga/clock-smoke/clock_smoke.v -- three PLLs, a four-clock liveness
#           panel on PMOD1, and a UART report carrying three rates and three
#           lock bits
# Built:    scripts/build-clock-smoke.sh
# Bitstream: 34,668,145 bytes
#          sha256 d563fa3c8685436bc8ba389247d270d0d9b364948962a7f7e80cc447e37ec798
# Loaded:   openFPGALoader -b tangconsole, one-wire (MCU port 0403:6010), SRAM,
#           erase + load to 100%, DONE, after a power cycle
# Captured: /dev/ttyUSB1 at 115200, 25 s -> 37 lines
# Observed: user report -- all four heartbeat LEDs blinking, in sync and IN PHASE

==================================================================
1. The panel: one lane per clock, each divider in its own domain
==================================================================

All eight PMOD1 lanes are driven.  Four are heartbeats, three are PLL locks, one
is a spare held high:

  LED 1  sys_clk   50.00 MHz  heartbeat, 1 Hz    (modulus 25,000,000)
  LED 2  clk27     27.00 MHz  heartbeat, 1 Hz    (modulus 13,500,000)
  LED 3  hclk      74.25 MHz  heartbeat, 1 Hz    (modulus 37,125,000)
  LED 4  clk_nes   21.50 MHz  heartbeat, 1 Hz    (modulus 10,750,000)
  LED 5  pll_27    LOCK       steady
  LED 6  pll_hdmi  LOCK       steady
  LED 7  pll_nes   LOCK       steady
  LED 8  spare                steady

The point of the design is that each heartbeat counter is clocked BY THE CLOCK IT
MEASURES.  A divider clocked by sys_clk would prove only that sys_clk is alive;
clocked in its own domain, the lane is a witness to that clock and to nothing
else -- if the PLL stops, its counter stops, its toggle flop stays where it
powered up, and the lane goes dark.

Each modulus is that clock's cycles in half a second at its NOMINAL frequency, so
each lane blinks at exactly 1 Hz when the clock is right, and the modulus is a
test rather than a convenience: a clock at twice its nominal rate blinks at 2 Hz.
That is what made this design the instrument that caught the decoder error in
section 2.

The three spares are driven HIGH rather than left floating, so nothing on the
panel is dark by accident: this module lights on HIGH, so ANY dark lane is a
fault, and which fault is read from the lane's position.  (The polarity is
measured, not assumed -- see clock-smoke-chaser.txt section 3, where driving one
lane low made it the dark one.)

==================================================================
2. The decoder was wrong by exactly 2x, and that is what "doubled" was
==================================================================

The design counts EDGES of bit 12 of each clock's counter.  Bit 12 has a PERIOD
of 2^13 = 8192 cycles and produces TWO edges per period, so a count of N edges
spans N x 4096 cycles of that clock.  tools/decode-clock-smoke.py multiplied by
8192 instead, on the docstring's reading that "a count of its edges is a count of
cycles".  Every rate it has reported since core-log entry 7 is therefore twice
the truth.

Three independent confirmations, none of them using the corrected script:

  * THE PANEL.  Four heartbeats, all at 1 Hz and in phase.  They blink at 1 Hz
    only if every clock is at its nominal rate, and in phase only if they share
    one frequency -- which they do, because every clock here is an exact rational
    multiple of the one 50 MHz crystal.  If clk27 were 54 MHz its lane would
    blink at 2 Hz and could not stay in phase.

  * HAND ARITHMETIC on the raw capture.  From consecutive lines, c27 advances
    0x1148 = 4424 and hk advances 0x2F76 = 12150.  For a line of 2^25 sys_clk
    cycles:  4424 x 4096 / 2^25 = 0.5400 x sys_clk, and 12150 x 4096 / 2^25 =
    1.4832.  The design asks for 0.5400 and 1.4851.

  * THE RECORDED NUMBER.  Decoded with the corrected factor, hclk reads 74.2452
    MHz -- which is the figure evidence/clock-smoke-readout.txt recorded, decoded
    BEFORE entry 7 changed this factor.  The original script was right.

The design's own SDC said so all along: `create_clock -name clk27 -period 37.037`
is 27 MHz, and the decoder was reporting 54 for the same net.  A constraint file
and a measurement contradicted each other for five core-log cycles and nobody
reconciled them.

WHAT IT OVERTURNS.  The committed design was never doubled.  And the ODIV
conclusion inverts: ODIV0_SEL is a straight divisor, so 50 divides by 50, which
is what the design asks for, and 100 halves the output to 13.5.  Entry 9's six
power-cycled variant-B runs, re-read through the corrected factor, say exactly
that.  So the desktop core's `ODIV0_SEL = 100` -- the workaround entries 10 and 11
landed -- is a 2x error: it halves pll_27 to 13.5 MHz, which halves pll_hdmi's
reference, which puts hclk5 at 185.6 MHz instead of 371.25.  That is half the
TMDS bit clock, and it is in the bitstream that was loaded on 2026-10-09.

It does NOT crack the display on its own: entry 11 compared two pixel rates and
the valid one was dark, so the pixel clock is not the cause.  What it means is
that the current desktop core carries TWO defects, and the known one should be
removed before the unknown one is tested.

==================================================================
3. The third PLL exists now, and it is measured rather than inferred
==================================================================

`pll_nes` was added on PLL_B[1] at X32Y108 -- the same site the desktop core's
own place-and-route used -- so this design holds the same trio the real core
does: PLL_L[1], PLL_L[3], PLL_B[1].  nextpnr reports `PLL: 3/12` placed.

MDIV 40 -> VCO 2000 MHz, ODIV0 93 -> 21.505 MHz, matching the desktop core's
gowin_pll_nes.v defparams exactly.

Until now that clock's correctness was INFERRED: the desktop core's keylink
answered coherently at a 2 Mbaud baud derived from it, which argues the clock is
right but measures nothing.  It is now read from its own counter.

==================================================================
4. The measurement: all three PLLs, all at their designed rates
==================================================================

  37 lines over 25 s, every one of them `lock=111`, no sample skipped:

    clk27    0.5400 x sys_clk  =  26.9982 MHz   designed 0.5400 -> 27.00
    hclk     1.4850 x sys_clk  =  74.2452 MHz   designed 1.4851 -> 74.25
    clk_nes  0.4301 x sys_clk  =  21.5040 MHz   designed 0.4300 -> 21.50

Three of three at their designed values, and the corrected script lands on them
-- so the correction is confirmed by the tool as well as by the panel and by
hand.  Both PLL lock bits this project could already see, and the third, are set.

The user reports the four heartbeat lanes blinking at 1 Hz and in phase, which is
the prediction and is also the sync question answered empirically: no drift,
because there is no second oscillator to drift against.

==================================================================
5. The hclk anomaly from the previous cycle, resolved and bounded
==================================================================

The chaser's two builds both reported hclk = 0 while this panel build and the two
pre-chaser builds report it alive.  Same logic, four placements, two outcomes,
with 0 route errors and timing PASS in all of them.

So it is placement-dependent, not design-dependent: the fabric fallback that
carries hclk5 to the CLKDIV can be made to fail by an unrelated change to the
I/O and a little sys_clk logic.  That is the display's failure in miniature, and
it is still un-explained in mechanism -- but it is bounded, reproducible, and
now measurable by a counter that either advances or does not.

==================================================================
6. An open defect in the readout, found while checking the panel
==================================================================

The `s=` field of every captured line reads 0, and it should alternate: it is
`cnt_sys[24]`, which toggles at 1.49 Hz.  The routed netlist says why -- `cnt_sys`
is declared `reg [24:0]` and comes back as 24 flop cells, not 25, so bit 24 is
gone and the field is stuck.

The RTL carries `(* keep *)` on that counter precisely because this happened
before, and its comment says the keep was the fix: "without it yosys trims a
counter whose top bit is the only one read -- cnt_sys[24] came back as 24 flops,
not 25 -- so cnt_sys[24] silently became bit 23 and the reported rate doubled".

The keep is evidently not holding for `cnt_sys`.  Nothing depends on the field
today -- clk27's and hclk's reports read `cnt_27[12]` and `cnt_hclk[12]`, and both
counters keep their bits -- but it is the same class of silent shift that cost
this project a factor of two before, and it is recorded rather than left as a
loose end.

==================================================================
7. Reproducing this, and what is next
==================================================================

  bash scripts/build-clock-smoke.sh                  # ~1 min
  # power-cycle the board first, always -- TOOLCHAIN.md, board side
  openFPGALoader -b tangconsole /home/vash/tools/clock-smoke/clock-smoke.fs
  stty -F /dev/ttyUSB1 115200 raw -echo
  timeout 25 cat /dev/ttyUSB1 > capture.bin
  python3 tools/decode-clock-smoke.py capture.bin --seconds 25

The decoder's message format changed with the design (58 bytes, `lock=DEF`, an
`ns=` field), so captures older than this cycle no longer parse.  That is
expected; the corrected factor was verified against a pre-change capture by hand.

Next, and it is the step the instrument exists for: the SWEEP.  Change one PLL's
divider, rebuild, and confirm the measured rate follows the request -- one PLL at
a time, with the UART as the number and the panel as the light.  That is what
"blink them any way I want" means, and it is a script now rather than an
investigation.  Then, and only then, the desktop core's `ODIV0_SEL` goes back to
50 before the HDMI is touched again, so a dark screen means something new.
