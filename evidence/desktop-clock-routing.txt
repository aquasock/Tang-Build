# The desktop core's clocks cannot be routed, which is why the display is dark
#
# Design:   nestang_top / the desktop core, via scripts/synth-desktop.sh and
#           scripts/pnr-desktop.sh
# Captured: /run/media/vash/GIT/TinyTang/build/desktop/reconstruct.jhpQys/
#           .open-pnr/nextpnr.log        (place and route, 2026-10-09 00:11)
#           .open-pnr/report.json        (same run)
# Hardware: the same core loaded over two-wire `tangload` and answering on
#           UART1 at 2000000 baud (evidence/desktop-2wire-cycle.txt), with the
#           HDMI display dark and the monitor reporting "no signal".

==================================================================
1. What the device's database gives this design
==================================================================

Read out of apycula's GW5AST-138C database with msgpack + lzma:

  pad_pll            0
  hclk_pips        171

The clock model IS present: this is the database regenerated from the local

  Info: constraining clock net 'clk' to 21.49 MHz

==================================================================
2. The routing that fails
==================================================================

947 lines of the form

  Warning: Failed to route net '<net>' from <src> to <dst> using dedicated routing.

break down by net as:

  clk      943
  hclk5      3
  clk27      1

Every one of the design's PLL-derived clocks fails dedicated routing.  The
three `hclk5` failures are the whole display, because `hclk5` is the TMDS bit
clock at 371.25 MHz and its only loads are the three output serialisers:

  Failed to route net 'hclk5' from X1Y81/MPLLCLKOUT0 to X181Y102/FCLKA
  Failed to route net 'hclk5' from X1Y81/MPLLCLKOUT0 to X181Y100/FCLKA
  Failed to route net 'hclk5' from X1Y81/MPLLCLKOUT0 to X181Y57/FCLKA

X181Y102, X181Y100 and X181Y57 are the three OSER10s, at the three TMDS data
pads (rows 58, 101 and 103 of column 182; the vendor's build places them at
the same three sites).  FCLKA is the serialiser's fast-clock input.  There is
no fourth, because the TMDS clock lane is a plain differential output of the
pixel clock and carries no serialiser.

==================================================================
3. Why no clock survives
==================================================================

  Info: 	                BUFG:       1/      1   100%

The architecture this build places against offers ONE global clock buffer for
the whole device, against a design that needs four clocks.  One of them can
take the global network; the rest are left to general routing.  A clock
carried by general fabric is good enough to count edges -- which is what
clock-smoke measured, and why its 74.2452 MHz reading was true and still did
not mean the clock was usable -- and it is not good enough to clock a
serialiser at 371.25 MHz.

This is coherent with everything else measured today, and each of those
readings stays valid: the PLL configuration is right (pll_27 at ODIV0_SEL=100
gives 26.9982 MHz and pll_hdmi at 4 gives 371.25 MHz, both reproduced 2/2 with
power cycles), the placement matches the vendor's at every clock and
serialiser cell, the pad configuration matches the vendor's tile by tile at
all four TMDS pins, and the TMDS tiles' serialiser attributes match the
vendor's apart from the LSRIMUX_0 that was fixed.  What fails is the delivery
of the clock to those cells, not its generation, its configuration, or its
destination.

==================================================================
4. What this does not establish
==================================================================

Which of the two clock-model gaps is load-bearing: the `hclk_pips` network
being too sparse to carry the path from the PLL at X1Y81 to the serialisers
at X181, or the single `BUFG` starving three of the four clocks of any global
route at all.  Both are missing or incomplete data for this device rather
than anything in the core, and both would have to be extended for the design
to place and route as the vendor's does.

The `pad_pll` key is 0 in this database as well, so the twelve PLL sites entry
1 of the core log recorded are carried under different keys; the PLLs place,
so whatever carries them works.

==================================================================
5. The clock model is more complete than this cycle assumed
==================================================================

This cycle went looking for the missing HCLK data and did not find it,
because most of it is not missing.  Four candidate gaps were tested against
the vendor's `.fse` and the database, and all four are refuted.  They are
recorded here so that none of them is proposed again.

  (a) "The interior horizontal spines are absent from the derived table."
      REFUTED.  The device has six HCLK blocks, and `gw5_hclk_idx` is a pure
      table lookup over exactly those six sites, returning -1 everywhere else:

          0:(27,0)  1:(27,181)  2:(81,0)  3:(81,181)  4:(108,64)  5:(108,117)

      The blocks carry 159 to 165 table-48 wire entries each.  Every interior
      tile tested -- row 81 at columns 36/64/90/117/145/168, row 108 at
      36/90/145/168, row 57 at 36/90/168 -- carries **0**.  The HCLK wiring
      lives inside the block tiles; there is no die-wide pip grid, so the
      predicate loses nothing by not visiting them.

  (b) "The blocks do not produce the HCLK lines."
      REFUTED, and the error was one of structure.  The lines are produced as
      `add_node` nodes, not as pips:

          dest = wnames.hclknames[30 + idx + hclk_idx * hclk_off]
          add_node(dev, f'HCLK{hclk_idx}_{dest}', "GLOBAL_CLK", row, col, src)

      Searching `hclk_pips` for them therefore returns nothing by
      construction.  `HCLK30` is hclknames index 657, i.e. 96 + 3*187.

  (c) "The producer and consumer wires are never joined."
      REFUTED.  `make_hclk_pip` registers both endpoints of every pip as
      nodes under the same name the producer uses:

          add_node(dev, f'HCLK{hclk_idx}_{src}',  "GLOBAL_CLK", row, col, src)
          add_node(dev, f'HCLK{hclk_idx}_{dest}', "GLOBAL_CLK", row, col, dest)

      so `create_global_nodes` joins the block at (81,181) to the IOLOGIC at
      (102,181) under `HCLK3_HCLK30`, and they become one routable node.

  (d) "The switch matrix is built only at the 171 `hclk_pips` sites, so the
      path crosses tiles that have no HCLK wiring."
      REFUTED by (c): the wires are joined by node identity, not by name
      coincidence, so the intervening tiles do not need their own matrix.

The paths this design needs are present, and the FCLK hooks exist at all
three serialisers:

    (102,181) lane 3:  FCLKA <- HCLK30, HCLK31, HCLK32, HCLK33
    (100,181) lane 3:  FCLKA <- HCLK30, HCLK31, HCLK32, HCLK33
    ( 57,181) lane 1:  FCLKA <- HCLK10, HCLK11, HCLK12, HCLK13

The PLL at X1Y81 is adjacent to block 2 at (81,0), so it feeds lane 2,
while the serialisers read lanes 3 and 1.

==================================================================
6. The live lead: lane 3's clock entry is a fabric wire
==================================================================

    _gw5_hclk_logic_entry_wires = ("CLK0", "CLK1", "CLK2", "LSR2")

Three of a block's four logic-to-HCLK entry wires are the tile's clock wires;
the fourth, `LSR2`, is an ordinary fabric wire.  The comment on that constant
records, MEASURED at `P1.T27` from the vendor's own bitstreams:

    what makes lane 3 different from lanes 0-2: a clock reaches it over
    fabric, never over the global plane

Two of this design's three TMDS data serialisers are on lane 3, and the PLL
feeds lane 2.  That predicts both the symptom (no usable 371.25 MHz clock at
the serialisers) and the router's own wording, `'hclk5' net was routed using
global resources partially` -- the last hop into lane 3 cannot be global.

NOT SETTLED, and deliberately not asserted: the vendor's build works at the
same three serialiser sites with the same forced PLL placement, so either the
reading of this comment is incomplete or the vendor flow has something this
one lacks.  The mechanism stays unestablished until that divergence is
explained.  It is the first candidate here that is measured rather than
inferred, and the inference is what has been wrong so far.

==================================================================
7. Why this cycle's hypotheses kept failing
==================================================================

All four refuted candidates failed the same way: a real number was read out
of the wrong structure.  The HCLK data is spread over four of them --
`hclk_pips` (fuse-bearing pips), `db.nodes` (routable nodes), the wire
namespace (names), and nextpnr's generated global node list -- and a datum
present in one is absent from another by construction.  Before inferring from
the absence of an HCLK datum, establish which structure holds it.

That is a note about method, not about the device: the fault is still real,
still in the open flow, and still exactly where it was.

==================================================================
8. The answer: the serialisers' fast clock is not in the bitstream
==================================================================

Entry 13's next step was to compare the HCLK mux state at the three
serialiser FCLK inputs, decoded from the vendor's bitstream and from ours.
That comparison was run and it localises the fault completely.

Our own synthesised netlist does connect FCLK.  All three OSER10s share one
net, 2025:

    nestang_top / u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer0 (OSER10)
        FCLK: [2025]      PCLK: [40]      Q: [7566]      RESET: [50]
    nestang_top / u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer1 (OSER10)
        FCLK: [2025]      PCLK: [40]      Q: [7577]      RESET: [50]
    nestang_top / u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer2 (OSER10)
        FCLK: [2025]      PCLK: [40]      Q: [7588]      RESET: [50]

(source: evidence/desktop-core-netlist.json.gz)

Both bitstreams unpacked with the same tool, same device, same three sites:

  vendor  R58C182:  .FCLK(R58C182_HCLK0)   .PCLK(R58C182_CLK0)
  vendor  R101C182: .FCLK(R101C182_HCLK0)  .PCLK(R101C182_CLK0)
  vendor  R103C182: .FCLK(R103C182_HCLK0)  .PCLK(R103C182_CLK0)

  ours    R58C182:  .PCLK(R58C182_CLK0)     -- no .FCLK
  ours    R101C182: .PCLK(R101C182_CLK0)    -- no .FCLK
  ours    R103C182: .PCLK(R103C182_CLK0)    -- no .FCLK

OUR BITSTREAM WAS RECORDED HERE AS HAVING NO FCLK CONNECTION AT ANY OF THE
THREE TMDS SERIALISERS.  THAT IS WRONG.  See section 11, which supersedes
sections 8 to 10: packing the routed netlist reproduces this exact bitstream
byte for byte, and unpacking it shows FCLK present at all three serialisers,
sourced from HCLK0, exactly as the vendor's does.
PCLK is present, which is why the core runs and its pixel-side logic works;
FCLK is the serialiser's shift clock, and without it the cell cannot emit a
bit, so there is no TMDS at all and the sink never locks.

The chain, each link evidenced separately:

  1. the design connects all three OSER10 FCLK inputs to one net, hclk5
     (net 2025)                          -- our netlist
  2. hclk5 is the 371.25 MHz TMDS bit clock and is generated correctly,
     2/2 reproducible with power cycles  -- clock-smoke divider runs
  3. nextpnr cannot route it to FCLKA at any of the three serialisers:
     3 of the 947 dedicated-routing failures, and
     "'hclk5' net was routed using global resources partially"
                                         -- .open-pnr/nextpnr.log
  4. therefore the connection never reaches the bitstream  -- this unpack
  5. the vendor's bitstream does have it, at the same three sites, from a
     Gowin build whose display works           -- this unpack
  6. no FCLK at the serialiser means no TMDS, which is the dark display

==================================================================
9. What this retires
==================================================================

The lane-3 lead of section 6 is NOT the cause.  The vendor connects FCLK at
the same three sites, so lane 3 is reachable and its fabric-only clock entry
does not prevent the connection.  It stays recorded as measured, and it is
not the blocker.

The chipdb is not implicated.  The arcs exist -- `FCLKA <- HCLK30..33` at
(102,181) and (100,181), `FCLKA <- HCLK10..13` at (57,181) -- and those sites
ARE in `hclk_pips`, so `create_hclk_switch_matrix` runs for them and creates
the pip.  nextpnr has the edge and still cannot route to it.

The remaining fault is one thing: the route from a PLL output through the
inter-HCLK network to an IOLOGIC's FCLK is not achievable in this nextpnr
build for this device, even though the graph contains the arcs.  That is a
routing/model defect rather than missing data, and it is where the next cycle
begins.

Method note.  What found this was the trick the packer's own comments had
already used twice -- compare our bitstream against Gowin's at a site where
both builds agree, and look for what is ABSENT rather than what differs.  The
pad configuration passed that comparison and so did the TMDS serialiser
attributes, which is exactly why the remaining gap was one layer up, in the
routing rather than in the configuration.

==================================================================
10. The route does not fail -- it is not packable
==================================================================

nextpnr exposes its own state after routing through a post-route Python hook
(`--post-route`).  himbaechel's binding is not the one the docs describe
(`ctx.nets` is an `IdNetMap` with no `keys()`), but it resolves: a net has
`driver`, `users` and `wires`, and a `PortRef` has `cell` and `port`.  Read
there, the picture is not the one sections 2 and 8 assumed.

    === net hclk5
      driver: pll_hdmi.PLL_inst.CLKOUT0   bel=X1Y81/PLL
      users: 4
          div5.HCLKIN                         bel=X181Y81/CLKDIV_3
          u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer0.FCLK  bel=X181Y102/IOLOGICAO
          u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer1.FCLK  bel=X181Y100/IOLOGICAO
          u_hdmi.hdmi.serializer.gwSer2.FCLK  bel=X181Y57/IOLOGICAO
      wires: 44, including X181Y102/FCLKA, X181Y100/FCLKA and X181Y57/FCLKA

ALL THREE SERIALISERS' `FCLKA` WIRES ARE IN THE NET.  The connection is
complete; what fails is the 3 warnings about dedicated routing, and nextpnr
then falls back and carries the net through general fabric instead.

Where it goes is the point.  Among the 44 wires are:

    X0Y0/HCLK31   X0Y0/HCLK11
    X181Y27/HCLK_MUX_BETA11   X181Y81/HCLK_MUX_BETA31
    X88Y54/SPINE16  X145Y64/SPINE25  X176Y77/GB10  X177Y73/GT00
    X176Y81/GB10  X179Y81/EW10 ...

`X0Y0` is NOT one of the 171 tiles in `hclk_pips`, so it has no HCLK switch
matrix and no HCLK pip.  The HCLK wire names nevertheless exist there, because
nextpnr creates them from `db.nodes` -- `create_global_nodes` walks every node
and calls `create_reuse_wire` at each of its positions -- so a wire whose name
is an HCLK resource is available at tiles where that resource is not.

Read against the bitstream: `gowin_pack` resolves the serialiser's clock
source from the HCLK structures, and a route carried by general fabric through
an HCLK wire name at a non-HCLK tile is not one it recognises, so no `FCLK`
mux is emitted.  Section 8's unpack showed exactly that -- no `FCLK` at any of
the three serialisers, where the vendor's build has one at all three.

ESTABLISHED, from nextpnr's own state: the net is routed to all four users,
all three `FCLKA` wires are in it, and 3 of the 947 dedicated-routing warnings
are this net's.  ESTABLISHED, from the unpack: the bitstream has no `FCLK` and
the vendor's does.  HYPOTHESIS, and labelled as one: that the packer drops a
`FCLK` driven over general fabric or over an HCLK wire at a tile outside
`hclk_pips`.  It is testable directly -- pack this routed JSON and unpack the
result, which is minutes and no board -- and it should be tested before any
fix is written, because the fix differs depending on which half of that
disjunction is true.

Two details worth keeping.  `clk27` shows the same shape: 1 user, 10 wires,
its single sink among the warnings -- routed, and presumably unpacked the same
way.  And `clk`, the 21.49 MHz NES clock, reports 2138 users and 2138 wires,
one wire per sink: it is routed as a star over general fabric rather than
through a clock spine, which is why the whole design works while the 371.25
MHz one does not.

11. Correction: the FCLK is present, and the bitstream reproduces exactly
==================================================================

The test named at the end of section 10 was run, and it refutes section 8.
Packing the routed netlist from the same run with `gowin_pack` and unpacking
the result gives:

  OSER10 R58C182:  .PCLK(R58C182_CLK0)  .FCLK(R58C182_HCLK0)
  OSER10 R101C182: .PCLK(R101C182_CLK0) .FCLK(R101C182_HCLK0)
  OSER10 R103C182: .PCLK(R103C182_CLK0) .FCLK(R103C182_HCLK0)

FCLK IS PRESENT at all three serialisers, sourced from `HCLK0`, which is what
the vendor's unpack shows too.  The packer does not drop it, and the
hypothesis at the end of section 10 -- that it discards an FCLK driven over
general fabric or over an HCLK wire outside `hclk_pips` -- is refuted.

What makes this decisive rather than another reading is that the pack
reproduces the loaded bitstream exactly:

  freshly packed .fs   sha256 ec6baf2a894a8b6c3f991874d969b27ff5bab391a26d8440d85efe39dc80b6f1
  loaded .fs, recorded sha256 ec6baf2a894a8b6c3f991874d969b27ff5bab391a26d8440d85efe39dc80b6f1

  freshly packed .bin  sha256 9b70a448dfc3f83c1c9d7afd02f0cab6c9fbf18824f12cb905d9badf0aa0f6e5
  staged   .bin, recorded sha256 9b70a448dfc3f83c1c9d7afd02f0cab6c9fbf18824f12cb905d9badf0aa0f6e5

The bytes that were on the board are the bytes that were just unpacked.  Two
consequences, one of them encouraging:

  * section 8's claim is wrong and the search for the display's cause restarts
    from a bitstream whose `FCLK` lines match the vendor's exactly.  What is
    left is what differs ELSEWHERE, not at the serialisers' clock port.

  * the bitstream is BYTE-REPRODUCIBLE.  `nextpnr` from the fork's `7ed099ec`
    plus `gowin_pack` from the fork's apicula plus the regenerated database

Why the earlier unpack read differently is not recoverable: `unpack-ours.v`
was in `/tmp` and is gone, so the file that produced it cannot be re-examined.
What is recorded above was verified twice over from surviving inputs -- pack
then unpack, and the hash of the pack against the recorded hash of the loaded
file -- and it is the reading to trust.

The corrected state of the question: the serialisers are clocked, the pad and
serialiser configuration matches the vendor's, the PLL frequencies are right,
and the bitstream is reproducible.  The display still does not come up, and
what differs from the vendor's build is now open again.

==================================================================
12. The first real difference: how lane 3's clock arrives
==================================================================

The HCLK block mux state was compared by wrapping apicula's own
`parse_hclk_block` and running the ordinary unpack over both bitstreams, so
both readings come from the decoder rather than from a reimplementation
(`/home/vash/tools/pnr-test/hclk-state.py`).  Exactly one block decodes to
anything in either bitstream -- `(81,181)`, the HCLK block for lane 3, which
serves two of this design's three TMDS serialisers -- and the two disagree.

OURS, block (81,181):

    CLKDIV_3: DIV_MODE="5"
    HCLK3:    HCLK_BUF_BI33="HCLK_MUX_BETA33"
              HCLK_MUX_ALPHA31="HCLK_BUF_BO31"
              HCLK_BUF_BI31="HCLK_MUX_BETA31"
              HCLK_MUX_ALPHA33="HCLK_BUF_BO33"
              HCLK_MUX_BETA31="L2HCLK31"
              HCLK_MUX_BETA33="L2HCLK33"

VENDOR, same block:

    CLKDIV_0: DIV_MODE="5"
    HCLK3:    HCLK_MUX_GAMMA30="HCLK_UNK581"
              HCLK_MUX_ALPHA30="HCLK_BUF_BO30"

TWO THINGS FOLLOW.  `L2HCLK` is the logic-to-HCLK entry -- a block whose clock
arrives from fabric -- and ours selects it for both BETA muxes, where the
vendor selects `HCLK_UNK581` on GAMMA.  `HCLK_UNK` is the band that carries the
inter-HCLK wires, the block-to-block traffic of the dedicated HCLK network
(`gw5_make_hclk_pips`' `_IHCLK` branch names its wires out of that band), so
the vendor's lane 3 is driven over the dedicated network while ours is driven
from general fabric.  Second, the CLKDIV slot differs -- `CLKDIV_3` in ours,
`CLKDIV_0` in the vendor's -- while both divisors read `DIV_MODE="5"`.

This is consistent with everything already measured and it explains the
software path end to end: nextpnr reports 3 dedicated-routing failures for
`hclk5`, falls back to general fabric, and `gowin_pack` faithfully encodes the
resulting fabric entry.  The bitstream is not lying about the route; the route
is one that cannot work at 371.25 MHz.  The serialisers' own `FCLK` and `PCLK`
are downstream of the lane and so are untouched, which is why they match the
vendor's and why the search kept coming back to them.

The block beside the PLL, `(81,0)` for lane 2, decodes to `{}` in BOTH
bitstreams, so that is not a difference and the search should not go there.

ESTABLISHED: the two bitstreams differ at `(81,181)` in which input lane 3's
clock mux selects, ours fabric and the vendor's inter-HCLK, and the CLKDIV slot
used.  INFERRED, not measured: that a fabric clock is what makes 371.25 MHz
unusable here, and therefore that a serialiser clocked from lane 3 gets a
smear rather than a bit clock.  The physics is sound and the vendor's choice
points the same way, but nothing here has put a probe on the pin.

CAVEAT ON THE REFERENCE.  RESOLVED by section 13.

13. Confirmed against the bitstream that drives a working display
==================================================================

The card itself is not mounted, but the bitstream it carries is in the tree.
Three files under `TinyTang/build/oled-terminal/` hash to
`4fcc62e6570805b4ea02fb7356c3344e7df90ad980bfe78c66475f1245439873`, which is
the `desktop.bin` on the SD card, and one of them sits in a full Gowin build
tree with its `.fs` beside it:

    TinyTang/build/oled-terminal/sweep-blockfix/reconstruct.qb3T82/
        impl/pnr/desktop.{bin,fs}        (.bin = 4fcc62e6..., 4,463,306 bytes)

The other two are `sweep-blockfix/place2/desktop.bin` and
`qualify/desktop-blockfix.bin`.  So the reference no longer depends on the card
and, more to the point, it is now the file that demonstrably drives a working
display rather than a build of unknown provenance.

Decoded with the same wrapped `parse_hclk_block`, its block at `(81,181)` is:

    CLKDIV_0: DIV_MODE="5"
    HCLK3:    HCLK_MUX_GAMMA30="HCLK_UNK581"
              HCLK_MUX_ALPHA30="HCLK_BUF_BO30"

IDENTICAL to the tree's other Gowin build and DIFFERENT from ours.  Two
independent Gowin builds agreeing with each other is what settles it: the
difference is not a build-to-build artifact, and it is not an artifact of
comparing against the wrong bitstream either.  Section 12's vendor reading was
of `source/impl/pnr/desktop.fs`, whose `.bin` is `c8406c7f...` and is a
different build from the card's `4fcc62e6...`; both decode to the same lane-3
state.  Every other block site decodes to `{}` in all three bitstreams,
including `(81,0)`, lane 2's block beside the PLL.

What this establishes, and what it does not.  ESTABLISHED: the working
bitstreams drive lane 3's clock over the dedicated HCLK network, selecting the
inter-HCLK wire `HCLK_UNK581`; ours selects `L2HCLK`, the logic-to-HCLK entry,
which is fabric; the CLKDIV slot differs likewise, `CLKDIV_0` against
`CLKDIV_3`; and nextpnr's 3 dedicated-routing warnings for `hclk5` are the
moment it gave up and fell back.  `gowin_pack` encoded that fallback
faithfully.  STILL INFERRED, NOT MEASURED: that a fabric clock is what makes
371.25 MHz unusable here.  The physics and now two vendor builds' choice both
point that way, but nothing here has put a probe on the pin, and the inference
should not be written up as the finding.
