==================================================================
1. What the variant changes (scripts/nopll-variant.sh)
==================================================================

  a. nestang_top.sv: the three PLL instantiations and the CLKDIV are
     replaced by assignments from the 50 MHz input:

       assign clk27 = sys_clk;   assign clk  = sys_clk;
       assign fclk  = sys_clk;   assign hclk5 = sys_clk;
       assign hclk  = sys_clk;

  b. iosys_bl616's frequency parameter, which its UART baud is divided
     from, is retargeted: .FREQ(21_492_000) -> .FREQ(50_000_000).
     (The keyboard link already names CLK_HZ(50_000_000).)

  c. desktop.sdc: from three clocks to one.  In this variant sys_clk,
     clk and hclk5 are the same net, and giving one net two periods
     makes nextpnr's clock handling fail -- it names the net it cannot
     route.  Measured both ways; this is the single-clock version:
       # Bring-up variant: one clock.  Every domain runs from the 50 MHz input.
       create_clock -name sys_clk -period 20 [get_nets {sys_clk}]

  Cost of the variant, stated plainly: HDMI cannot work (TMDS needs
  371.25 MHz exactly) and the NES core runs 2.33x fast.  Kept: the
  desktop, its register interface, the OLED terminal.

6. Why -- the chip's clocking is the part that is not modelled
==================================================================

  a. The device database has no clock structures for this chip.  Read
     out of apycula/*.msgpack.xz with msgpack + lzma:

       device         pad_pll   hclk_pips   io2hclk   hclk_div2 
       GW5A-25A       25        247         4         4         
       GW5AST-138C    0         0           0         0         

  b. The whole device reports one global clock buffer.  nextpnr, this
     run: 'BUFG: 1/1 100%'.  The vendor build of this same design
     (build/desktop/place2/desktop.rpt.txt, section 5) reports
     'PRIMARY 4/8', 'GCLK_PIN 3/24', 'CLKDIV 1/24', and section 6 shows
     hclk5 distributed on 'HCLK BANK3_HCLK0'.

  c. Scale.  Upstream's own designs for this die, which do route:

       uart-message-tangmega138k.json      177 cells
       attosoc-tangmega138k.json         3,328 cells   (1,119 LUT4, 545 DFF)
       nestang_top, this variant         8,730 cells   (2,262 LUT4+2,202 LUT1..3, 2,414 DFF)

     This core has ~4x the clock loads of anything that has been routed
     on this device, and the clock has to reach correspondingly more of
     the die.  That is consistent with, but not proof of, the clock
     network's coverage being the limit rather than the design.

==================================================================
7. What this does and does not establish
==================================================================

  Established: the design is placeable on this die with the open tools
  once the clock primitives are removed from it, and the remaining
  obstacle is routing the clock -- not the PLL bel, and not anything
  about the logic, IO, BSRAM, DSP or IOLOGIC.

  Not established: no bitstream was produced, nothing was packed with
  gowin_pack, and nothing was loaded.  Whether a smaller variant (a
  desktop-only core without the NES) would fit inside the clock
  network's coverage is an untested hypothesis, and it is the most
  promising workaround left.

  Also worth an upstream report on its own: nextpnr aborts with
  std::out_of_range instead of reporting that it cannot route a clock
  net, and the 1 BUFG figure for a device whose vendor build uses four
  primary clocks looks like missing data rather than a small clock tree.
