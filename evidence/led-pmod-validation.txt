# The PMOD LED socket, validated

Purpose: to know what these eight lights can and cannot express, so that the LED
panel can be used as a **readout of the clock tree** while the display is worked
on.  This is the instrument that gets carried into the HDMI work.

Validated build: `/home/vash/tools/clock-smoke/clock-smoke.fs`, sha256
`47ea82c309d87697d9656f72e06b03ca88be7747f3d5aaff8ff1c94e2218018b`.
All eight lanes pass on the board.

## The panel

```
LED 1  sys_clk   50.000 MHz  a heartbeat, clocked by sys_clk
LED 2  clk27     27.000 MHz  a heartbeat, clocked by clk27
LED 3  hclk      74.250 MHz  a heartbeat, clocked by hclk
LED 4  clk_nes   21.505 MHz  a heartbeat, clocked by clk_nes
LED 5  pll_27    lock        a 107 ms echo of the beat, 550 ms in
LED 6  pll_hdmi  lock        the same echo, 700 ms in
LED 7  pll_nes   lock        the same echo, 850 ms in
LED 8  sys_clk   spare       a slow triangle, ~4 s up and down
```

Each heartbeat lane is clocked by the clock it reports on, so a lane is a
witness to that clock and nothing else.  Each echo lane is gated by its own
lock at the pin, so an unlocked PLL is dark rather than lying.  A lane that
goes dark or stops moving is therefore a statement about a clock, not about the
panel.

## What the lights can do

**Brightness is real but compressed at the top.**  Measured by eye on four
steady lanes at 6, 25, 50 and 100 per cent duty, a human rates them 70, 80, 90
and 100 per cent bright.  The whole top of the duty range collapses into the top
third of the perceived range.

**And it does not matter, because a fade from off to on reads as a full
excursion at any peak.**  The spare lane's four-second triangle at a 6.2 per
cent peak duty is reported as fading "in and out from 0-100% properly".  A duty
ladder (peaks of 1.6, 3.1, 6.2 and 12.4 per cent) was built to chase the
compression and was a dead end: the variable that matters is not the duty range.

**Time is the binding constraint**, and this was the whole difficulty.  A
brightness change has to outlast the eye's integration window to be seen as a
change at all.  The first envelope decayed over 64 ms inside a single 107 ms
sound, and four perfectly correct lanes read as nothing but on and off.  Stretch
the decay to 255 ms and the fade is visible; that is the delivered shape.

**Carrier rate is not a demonstrated constraint.**  Something between about
200 Hz and about 1.1 kHz is known to work -- the steady 6 per cent probe at
200 Hz reads dim, the breath at about 763 Hz reads correctly, and the echo
pings at about 412 Hz read correctly.  The upper limit is NOT established: the
original build ran a 195 kHz carrier and looked flat, but that was the decay,
and a carrier ladder from 195 kHz down to 100 Hz changed nothing, so no test
has ever isolated the carrier.  Do not claim a limit that was not measured.

**One probe was misread and is recorded so it is not repeated.**  A 1 Hz carrier
at 6 per cent duty was taken as proof that the LED displays duty.  It is on for
62 ms once a second: a statement about *timing*, not about brightness.  The
probe that answers the brightness question is a *steady* dim light beside steady
bright ones.

## Two defects this panel found

**A reset-less FSM is re-encoded one-hot and comes up dead.**  Detailed in
`evidence/clock-smoke-heartbeat-visible.txt`; it is why the first heartbeat test
was four dark lanes.  One attribute, `(* fsm_encoding = "none" *)`, fixes it.

**A delayed lane blinked once and died.**  The state machine's second-sound gate
is `phase >= S2_ONSET + DELAY_MS`, and with the second sound switched off a lane
whose delay pushes that sum past the end of the second -- past 597 ms -- never
leaves `WAIT2`.  Lanes delayed 700 and 850 ms sat dead while the 550 ms lane
worked.  Found on the board; the fix returns straight to the start when S2 is
off.  The regression now runs **two beats** and checks the second onset, and it
was verified to fail against the old code rather than assumed to catch it -- the
first version of that check used a 550 ms delay, which is inside the second and
therefore passed on the broken design.

## What this buys the HDMI work

The three PLLs now each have a lane that beats from their own clock, a lock
flag, and a UART readout that reports the envelope's ramp directly in the `s=`
field of the message.  A clock that stops, drifts, or loses lock is visible on
the board without an instrument, and the message distinguishes a real ramp from
a stepped one.  Keep the lane meanings and keep power-cycling before every load:
an SRAM image loaded onto a running design does not take effect and produces
plausible, meaningless readings.
