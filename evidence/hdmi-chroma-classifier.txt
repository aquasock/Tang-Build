hdmi-frames.py: classifying a capture by chroma
==============================================

Cycle: 2026-10-10, entry 70.  Tool: tools/hdmi-frames.py.  Board: none needed --
this is a change to an instrument, verified against captures already on disk.

The defect
----------

The classifier decided a frame's kind from luma alone:

    if max(ys) <= 16 and min(ys) >= 16:
        kind = "LEGAL BLACK ..."
    elif min(ys) == 0 and max(ys) > 96:
        kind = "CARD LOGO (bright text on background 0 -- not legal video)"

The second rule is satisfied by any dark-background picture with bright pixels,
which is a signature a real colour picture shares with the card's own on-screen
display.  In this project it labelled Phosphor's colourful visualiser as the
card's logo, and it labelled the open build's monochrome capture the same way.
Two wrong readings were drawn from it before the chroma was checked by hand:
that the Tang's HDMI emitted a valid signal carrying legal black, and that the
card's logo was a picture.  The tool printed the chroma it measured and did not
use it.

The fix
-------

Chroma decides.  A real picture varies in chroma; the card's flat idle field,
its "No Video" overlay and a source sending legal black are all monochrome,
with chroma pinned at 128 in every pixel.  So chroma is tested first, and luma
only then describes the frame:

    n_chroma = len(set(cs))
    if n_chroma > 2:            -> PICTURE
    elif luma 16 throughout:    -> NO PICTURE, flat monochrome field
    elif black with bright:     -> NO PICTURE, the card's No Video overlay
    else:                       -> NO PICTURE, monochrome, chroma flat

The docstring was rewritten to say why, and to record that the old luma rule
mislabelled a working capture, so the mistake is not inherited.

Result
------

The two known-good captures classify as PICTURE and the known-bad one as NO
PICTURE in every frame, which is the separation the old luma rule could not
make.  The instrument now agrees with the reading that was established by
entry 69's controlled experiment: the vendor flow delivers colour, the open
flow does not.
