==================================================================
1. The rebuild is byte-identical to the pre-change build, which was not expected
==================================================================

The hex digest above is the one evidence/clock-smoke-readout.txt recorded before
any of this cycle's changes, so the same design, built again through the
repaired script against the changed device database and a nextpnr rebuilt from
it, produced the same 35.7-million-bit file.  A prediction recorded earlier in
this cycle -- that the hash would necessarily change because the database and
the architecture had -- was WRONG, and the measurement is the better answer.

The reason is worth keeping: the change to apycula constrains which HCLK lanes
an IOLOGIC's fast clock may come from (patches/0003), and this design contains
no OSER10 and therefore no IOLOGIC that selects a lane at all.  The removed
arcs were never on its router's path, so its placement, its routing and its
packed output are untouched.  That makes the change behaviour-preserving for a
design with no IOLOGIC FCLK, demonstrated rather than argued -- and it also
means the desktop core's dark display cannot be blamed on the edit perturbing
placement.

==================================================================
2. The UART report: the design runs, and both PLLs lock
==================================================================

Two captures, decoded with tools/decode-clock-smoke.py:

  capture   window   lines   d(n)          PLL lock bits
  1         31.0 s   46      {1: 45}       11 x46
  2         15.0 s   23      {1: 22}       11 x23

Both report `lock=11` on every line, both use every sample and skip none, and
every line-counter step is exactly one line period.  sys_clk, from the UART
anchor of 434 x 115200 baud, is 49.9968 MHz in both -- the 50 MHz crystal.

  clk27   1.0800 x sys_clk  (53.9967 MHz)   capture 1
          1.0800 x sys_clk  (53.9966 MHz)   capture 2
  hclk    2.9701 x sys_clk  (148.4947 MHz)  capture 1
          2.9700 x sys_clk  (148.4907 MHz)  capture 2

The two runs agree to about three parts in 10^5, and the second is not merely a
re-measurement: at line n=0007 the counters read `c27=7907 hk=4cd1` in both,
the same values at the same line index, so the design is deterministic from
reset across separate loads.

clk27 and hclk both read DOUBLE their design values, and that is not a fault of
this cycle.  `fpga/clock-smoke/clock_smoke.v` carries
`defparam pll_27.ODIV0_SEL = 50`, and evidence/clock-smoke-divider-runs.txt
records that on this die the request is effectively halved: 50 divides by 25
where 100 divides by 50.  So 54 MHz and 148.5 MHz are what this committed
design is supposed to report, `pll_hdmi`'s separate divider is correct in the
same runs, and hclk5 follows at 742.5 MHz rather than 371.25.  The desktop
core's own pll_27 does not carry that value: its netlist asks for ODIV0_SEL=100,
the workaround the earlier cycles landed.

==================================================================
3. The LED observation, and what it confirms
==================================================================

CORRECTED: the polarity conclusion below is WRONG.  A later measurement with
clock-smoke driving one lane at a time shows this module lights on HIGH, not
low -- see `evidence/clock-smoke-chaser.txt` section 3 for the measurement and
for what the correction implies.  The mapping table and the lane-order reading
below are unaffected and stand.

clk_smoke's lane order is Digilent pin order, lanes 0-7 being module pins
1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 10 (Tang-Phosphor src/pmod/pmod_slot.sv), and
`pmod_slot.sv` interleaves that onto the dock's IO numbering as IO0/2/4/6 on
lanes 0-3 and IO1/3/5/7 on lanes 4-7.  Against what this design drives:

  lane  module pin  what drives it          LED   observed
  0     1           pmod1_io0 = cnt_sys[24]  1    BLINK  (~1.49 Hz)
  1     2           pmod1_io2 = cnt_hclk[24] 2    BLINK  (~4.43 Hz)
  2     3           pmod1_io4 = lock_hdmi    3    dark
  3     4           pmod1_io6, not driven    4    dark
  4     7           pmod1_io1 = cnt_27[23]   5    BLINK  (~3.22 Hz)
  5     8           pmod1_io3 = lock27       6    dark
  6     9           pmod1_io5, not driven    7    dark
  7     10          pmod1_io7, not driven    8    dark

The three blinking LEDs are exactly the three counter lanes, in module order,
INCLUDING the non-adjacent lane 4 read as LED5 -- a set that cannot match by
accident.  IF the module's LEDs run in lane order, as Digilent's 8-LED Pmod
does with its LD0..LD7, then this is the first hardware confirmation of the
dock's interleave, which until now came from Tang-Phosphor's records and the
schematic.  The module in use was not identified, and that assumption is what
the reading rests on; it is the one thing to close before calling the mapping
measured.

The two dark LEDs are the two pins the design holds STEADY HIGH, the PLL lock
bits.  That makes this module active-LOW: an LED lights when its pin is driven
low, so those two are dark precisely because both PLLs are locked.  It is the
same fact the UART reports as `lock=11`, read by eye instead of by tool.

It also explains the power-on state the user reported -- all eight LEDs lit
with nothing loaded -- because a ball with no design on it reads low, so an
active-low module lights all eight.  An expectation recorded earlier in this
cycle, that the three undriven lanes would stay lit and five would be steady,
was the OPPOSITE polarity and was wrong; the user's observation corrected it.

==================================================================
4. What this establishes, and what it does not
==================================================================

Establishes: a bitstream built by this repository's own tools runs on this
board, has now been seen to drive eight external LEDs, and reports its own
clocks and both PLL lock bits over a UART link, twice, with the two runs
agreeing to three parts in 10^5.

Establishes, as a by-product: the lane constraint in patches/0003 is
behaviour-preserving for a design with no IOLOGIC FCLK -- the same bitstream,
byte for byte.

Does NOT establish: anything about the HDMI display.  This design drives no
TMDS and its screen output is blank by construction; it is a clock instrument,
not a display test.  The desktop core's dark display remains open, with the
fabric-versus-dedicated clock path and the serialiser attribute difference both
unresolved -- see sections 18 and 19 of desktop-clock-routing.txt.

Does NOT establish: that the dock's interleave is measured, until the LED
module's own ordering is confirmed (section 3).

Does NOT establish: the three blink rates as measured.  They follow from the
counters and the doubled clock rates (1.49, 3.22 and 4.43 Hz at the design's
divider values), and the user read the three as similar rates with phase
offsets rather than as rates 2-3x apart.  Worth a re-look, not a finding yet.

==================================================================
5. Reproducing this
==================================================================

  bash scripts/build-clock-smoke.sh            # ~1 min, leaves the .fs in
                                               # /home/vash/tools/clock-smoke
  openFPGALoader -b tangconsole /home/vash/tools/clock-smoke/clock-smoke.fs
  stty -F /dev/ttyUSB1 115200 raw -echo
  timeout 31 cat /dev/ttyUSB1 > capture.bin
  python3 tools/decode-clock-smoke.py capture.bin --seconds 31

The load needs the MCU port, i.e. the one-wire arrangement, and is volatile.
An 8-LED PMOD in PMOD1, the socket beside the HDMI port, shows the result: three
blinking at three different rates and five dark, the two lock lanes among them.
