The HDMI instrument, validated with a control: Phosphor's picture and audio come
through, the desktop build's does not

2026-10-10.  No source, chipdb or bitstream was changed for any of this; the two
cores already existed and were loaded with `tangload`.

## Why a control was needed

Two readings in this session were taken from `tools/hdmi-frames.py`'s label for
a captured frame rather than from the frame itself, and both turned out to be
wrong.  The first was that the Tang's HDMI emits "a valid TMDS signal carrying
legal black"; the second read seed 19 the same way.  A capture card is a TMDS
receiver, but a receiver with no lock still hands over frames -- its own idle
field and its own "no video" overlay -- so "the card delivered frames" is not by
itself evidence that the Tang sent anything.

The user also asked the right question directly: *it might just be the capture
card's logo indicating no signal.*

## The control: Phosphor, which is known to drive this board's HDMI

Same board, same cable, same MACROSILICON card (`345f:2133`), same PC, minutes
apart.  `/cores/console138k/phosphortang.bin` loaded (`core 80`) and playing
`/music/01 - Ha Újra Látom.flac`:

    video   60 frames captured; luma 7 distinct, **chroma 22 distinct**
            (u 32..207, v 15..128) -> a COLOUR picture
    user    "A looks perfect.  i see the console."
    audio   5 s of 48 kHz stereo recorded from the card's USB audio input
            (ALSA card 2 "Guermok USB3 Video", device 0): peak 0.0 dBFS,
            rms -11.0 dBFS, per-100 ms rms varying 5,041..11,389
    user    "the audio is perfect.  i listened to the .wav file you made."

So the card, the cable and this PC are proven good end to end, for video and for
audio, and the board's HDMI output drives them when the right core is loaded.

## The desktop build, fresh boot, HDMI into the card

`desktop.bin` = seed 17, 120 frames (about 2 s at 60 fps):

    59 frames from   0   monochrome flat      luma exactly 16, chroma 128
    61 frames from  59   monochrome overlay   luma varies,     chroma 128

**Chroma is pinned at 128 in every one of the 120 frames.**  No colour is ever
present, so the card is never handing over a picture; the two phases are its own
idle field and its own overlay.  This agrees with the user's own observation on
a monitor -- "hmdi never comes up.  stays asleep" -- and with the coarse capture
taken before it, and it is the opposite of Phosphor's 22 chroma values.

## The discriminator is chroma, and the tool does not use it

A real picture varies in chroma.  The card's monochrome overlay and its flat idle
field both sit at exactly 128.  That is the test, and it separates the two cases
in one number.

`tools/hdmi-frames.py` instead classifies **any** frame with a dark background
and bright pixels as `CARD LOGO` -- and in this very session it labelled
Phosphor's colourful visualizer that way, because a real dark-background picture
has the same luma signature as the card's OSD.  That defect has now produced two
wrong conclusions, and it is recorded here rather than fixed in place, because it
is a change to a tool and not part of this measurement.  Its docstring's own
advice -- check which kind of frame you have before concluding anything -- is
right; the implementation cannot yet tell them apart.

## What stands, and what is withdrawn

- **Stands**: the desktop core's HDMI delivers no picture.  That is now measured
  with a validated instrument, not inferred from a monitor's behaviour, and it is
  consistent with the model: `hclk5`, the 371.25 MHz TMDS bit clock whose period
  is 2.6936 ns, fails dedicated routing at all four of its sinks with 2.759 ns of
  skew.
- **Stands**: VGA renders perfectly on this build -- the user sees the console on
  it right now -- so the defect is the TMDS half and not the compositor.
- **Withdrawn**: the verbal claim that the Tang's HDMI "emits a valid signal
  carrying legal black".  That was the card's idle state wearing a legal-black
  label.  It was never written into an entry; it is withdrawn here so it is not
  inherited.
- **Unchanged**: entries 62 and 67 concluded "no signal" and "HDMI blank" for the
  desktop core.  Both are correct; what was wrong was my reading of the capture,
  not those conclusions.

## Limits

The card's lock state during the flat frames is not determined -- a flat
monochrome field is ambiguous between "no lock" and "locked black" -- but no
picture is delivered either way, so nothing about the conclusion turns on it.
The desktop core has no audio source, so HDMI audio is not expected of it and its
absence there says nothing.  Phosphor's control proves the *instrument*; it does
not by itself prove that every Tang core's HDMI works, only that the path to this
PC does.
