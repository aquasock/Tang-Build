# The VGA lines: seven probes, and the design sits on a timing knife-edge

Cycle 2026-10-10 (second board session of the day). Base: Tang-Build `4905a20`.
Board attached; the user ran the display tests and I drove the loads.

This cycle set out to find which *change* put the vertical lines on the VGA. It
tested seven candidates on the board and closed all seven. What it found instead
is that the design has no timing margin, and that the lines are the price of the
one placement that has any.

## The known-good reference is real

`evidence/desktop-open.bin` (`fadd00ba...`, 4,463,286 B) renders **TinyDesk
perfectly** in both console and desktop mode on the user's Dell E772c — the same
text-and-glyph desktop that shows the lines today, on the same CRT, at the same
mode. So the glyph path is not the fault, and neither is the monitor.

## The seven probes

Every build below is the current design; only the named thing differs. Violation
counts are from `tools/pnr-timing.py`; the outcome is what the user saw.

| probe | hold violations | display |
|---|---|---|
| baseline: seed 23, `pll_nes` pinned `PLL_R[0]` | **0** | boots, **lined** |
| seed 59 | 1 | **dead** |
| vendor PLL site `PLL_B[1]` (X32Y108) | 5 | **dead** |
| `desktop_pmod.sv` reverted pre-`16b49b3` | 10 | **dead** |
| PLL sites unpinned (placer's choice) | 6 | not loaded |
| OLED terminal removed from `desktop_pmod` | 6 | not packed (2b refused) |
| eight-seed sweep (7,11,13,17,19,23,29,31) | 0 only at seed 23 | — |

**Every change breaks the timing-clean state.** Placement, netlist and design all
tip it into hold violations, and every build with violations gives a dead
display. The only configuration that reaches zero violations is the baseline —
and that is the lined one.

## What is not the cause (each tested, each closed)

- **The mode.** The monitor reports H 44.9 kHz / V 59.9 Hz for *both* the perfect
  and the lined build. Identical.
- **The PLL site.** Reverting to the vendor's own site kills the display.
- **The output phase.** Reverting `16b49b3`'s registered `visible` changed the
  netlist, so the placer re-ran and landed on 10 violations — the comparison is
  confounded and does not isolate the phase. Withdrawn as a suspect, not proven
  either way.
- **The OLED terminal.** Removing it makes timing *worse* (0 -> 6 violations).
- **Clock skew as the lines' cause.** The pixel clock's routed skew is **0.209 ns
  in every build**, pinned or unpinned, clean or violating — it is simply not
  placement-sensitive. The vendor site carries *more* `clk` skew (5.606 ns vs
  4.389) and is the site the user remembers as good.
- **Gate contention and gate reach** (previous cycles), and the spanning-pip and
  BUFG-insertion attempts before them.

## What this means

`clk` rides **general fabric** with **4.389 ns of skew** — 33% of a pixel period
— because the chipdb's clock network is not modelled. That is the same gap that
keeps the TMDS bit clock off the dedicated network and kills HDMI. The desktop
design has grown since the build that renders perfectly: **`fpga/desktop` gained
~878 lines** (the OLED terminal as new files, a rewritten `desktop_regs.sv`, and
the pmod timing change), and the fabric-clocked paths no longer tolerate the
skew. The good build was smaller and got away with it.

So the lines are a **symptom of a clock that is not where it should be**, and the
knife-edge is why every previous round circled: there is no placement to find,
only dead or lined.

## Measurements and tools added this session

- `tools/clock-skew-report.py` — a post-route hook reporting the *real* routed
  skew of every clock net. `tools/pnr-timing.py` prints `clk-skew` only inside
  violation blocks, so a passing build reports 0.000, meaning *unmeasured*.
- `tools/sweep-desktop-bins.sh` — builds, packs, converts and **content-stamps** a
  batch of placement candidates, so a candidate that reaches the screen can be
  identified afterwards.
- `scripts/build-desktop-core.sh` now stamps every build `desktop.<12-hex-of-its-
  own-sha256>.bin` / `.fs` and emits the `.bin` at all. This exists because
  `fadd00ba` had no recorded provenance and cost a cycle to fail to reconstruct.

## Limits

- Quantities in the table are from the runs named; the pre-commit and unpinned
  probes re-placed the design, so their violation counts are *placement* results,
  not clean isolations of the change.
- No build with hold violations was ever seen to produce a picture, so the claim
  "violations imply a dead display" rests on four observations, not a mechanism.
- The skew theory of the lines is *untested*, not refuted: no high-skew build has
  produced a visible picture to compare.

## Next

Model the HCLK interconnect in apicula's chipdb from the vendor's `.dat`/`.fse`.
It is the project's long-standing root cause, it is the only fix that is not a
workaround, and it is what would give both the main clock and the TMDS bit clock
a dedicated route. The acceptance measure is already built: the count of
`Failed to route net 'clk'` falling from 943, and `tools/clock-route-inventory.py`
reporting clocks on the dedicated network.
