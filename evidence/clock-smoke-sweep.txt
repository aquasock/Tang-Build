# Built:    scripts/build-clock-smoke.sh
# Bitstream: 34,668,145 bytes
#          sha256 57dcef1c9ba269db1b2b0537a3d53022656c138f9f652a3445b06f2b6d01fd9a
# Loaded:   openFPGALoader -b tangconsole, one-wire, SRAM, after a power cycle

==================================================================
1. Why this is the first sweep step
==================================================================

The goal is to show a PLL takes a divider and produces the frequency asked for.
`ODIV0_SEL` is the field whose interpretation this project got wrong for five
core-log entries: entries 8 and 9 read it as one that HALVES the requested
divisor, on the strength of a decoder that was itself multiplying every rate by
two.  With that factor removed, the recorded variant-B runs say the field is a
STRAIGHT DIVISOR, so 100 must halve the output.

That is exactly the value the desktop core carries as a workaround, so the step
is both the sweep's first rung and a test of a live defect.

==================================================================
2. The drift the user saw, and what it was
==================================================================

Before this build the user reported that lane 4 walked against lanes 1-3: a
small fast phase shift, no longer in sync after two or three minutes, while
1-3 stayed locked.  They also estimated the beat at "maybe an hour" and said
they would have liked to catch it but it was too slow to watch.

They were right, and it was this project's arithmetic rather than the PLL's.
`pll_nes` has a 2000 MHz VCO and a divider of 93, so it produces 21.505376 MHz
and cannot produce a round 21.5.  The modulus had been written for 21.5.

Three ways agreed, and a fourth settled it:

  * the user's eye:  lane 4 drifting against 1-3, beat ~1 hour
  * arithmetic:      21.505376/21.5 - 1 = 250 ppm -> 1/250e-6 = 4000 s = 67 min
  * a 180 s capture: lanes 1, 2 and 3 all blink at 0.999936 Hz, lane 4 at
                     1.000185 Hz -> +249.2 ppm -> beat 0.2492 mHz -> 66.9 min
  * the ratios:      clk_nes measured within -0.8 ppm of its designed ratio, so
                     the PLL is exact and the drift had to be the modulus

That last one is the decisive one.  The measured ratios are REFERENCE-FREE --
both the clock and sys_clk scale with the same crystal, so its error cancels --
which is what makes this a PLL measurement rather than a crystal measurement:

  clk27    +0.2 ppm from its designed ratio
  hclk     -0.3 ppm
  clk_nes  -0.8 ppm

All three at the limit of what 398 lines resolve.

And the shared explanation for lanes 1-3 standing still: their nominals are
exact and the crystal's -65 ppm error is common to all three, and a common error
is a fixed phase offset rather than a drift.  Only lane 4 had an error the others
did not share.

A side result worth keeping: the 1 Hz panel resolved a 249 ppm difference by eye
over two or three minutes.  An earlier reading in this project dismissed 1 Hz
LEDs as far too coarse for anything small and prescribed a TIA for the work; the
eye is better than that, and a few hundred ppm -- which is where integer dividers
actually land -- is visible.

==================================================================
3. The fix
==================================================================

Lane 4's modulus is now 10,752,688 cycles, half a second at the 21.505376 MHz the
divider can actually produce, and the comment above it says why.  A modulus taken
from a requested frequency instead of an achievable one is a deliberate drift,
and it belongs in a test rather than in a default.

Lanes 1 and 4 now compute to the same blink rate, 0.999936 Hz, so they hold
phase.

==================================================================
4. The measurement: halved exactly, and the independent PLL untouched
==================================================================

  90 lines over 60 s, every one of them `lock=111`, no sample skipped:

    clk27    0.2700 x sys_clk  =  13.4992 MHz   was 0.5400 / 26.9982
    hclk     0.7425 x sys_clk  =  37.1226 MHz   was 1.4850 / 74.2452
    clk_nes  0.4301 x sys_clk  =  21.5040 MHz   UNCHANGED

`ODIV0_SEL = 100` halves `pll_27`'s output, and `pll_hdmi` follows because its
reference is clk27: its VCO lands at 742.5 MHz, still inside the fitted 650-1300
band, and it locks -- all 90 lines say so.  `pll_nes` did not move at all, which
is the tree's dependency structure shown on hardware: it runs from the crystal,
not from clk27.

One divider changed, exactly the two clocks downstream of it moved by exactly the
predicted factor, and the independent one did not.  That is the sweep working and
the shape of it is the answer to "drive them any way I want".

The user reports all four heartbeat lanes locked and holding, with the drift gone.

==================================================================
5. What it means for the desktop core
==================================================================

The desktop core carries `ODIV0_SEL = 100` -- the workaround entries 10 and 11
landed on the strength of the decoder error.  Measured here, that value halves
its `pll_27` to 13.5 MHz, which halves `pll_hdmi`'s VCO to 742.5 MHz, which puts
its **hclk5 at 185.625 MHz instead of 371.25** -- half the TMDS bit clock.  It is
in the bitstream loaded on 2026-10-09.

Entry 11's comparison of two pixel rates still stands and the pixel clock is still
not the display's cause; what this does is remove a known 2x defect from a core
that is carrying it, before the unknown one is tested again.  One number: 100
goes back to 50.

==================================================================
6. Reproducing, and what is open
==================================================================

  bash scripts/build-clock-smoke.sh
  # power-cycle the board first, always -- TOOLCHAIN.md, board side
  openFPGALoader -b tangconsole /home/vash/tools/clock-smoke/clock-smoke.fs
  stty -F /dev/ttyUSB1 115200 raw -echo ; timeout 60 cat /dev/ttyUSB1 > cap.bin
  python3 tools/decode-clock-smoke.py cap.bin --seconds 60

Open, and small:

  * the CASCADE ORDER the user reported, lanes 3 then 2 then 1, is not what the
    clock tree implies -- sys_clk should start first, having no PLL to lock, then
    clk27, then hclk after two PLLs and the CLKDIV.  A mirrored numbering is ruled
    out by the chaser's in-order 1-to-8 walk, so it is something else.  The UART
    cannot see phase, so this needs either a phase instrument or a deliberate
    test, and it is not blocking anything.
  * the sweep's remaining rungs: other dividers, other PLLs, and a deliberately
    fast beat -- a 1% offset turns the 67-minute beat into about ten seconds and
    makes the arithmetic visible in one glance.
