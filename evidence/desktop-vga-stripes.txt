# VGA vertical stripes: identical-routing white control

Cycle date: 2026-10-09, America/Phoenix.

## Symptom and controlled board result

The user reports a stable desktop, console and Bluetooth across repeated cold
boots, but vertical dark lines across the VGA picture on a Dell E772c CRT.
The monitor reports H 44.9 kHz and V 59.9 Hz. The user reports that the picture
was clean before the PLL placement/seed timing change of core-log entry 41.

This cycle compared the running image with a constant-white derivative of its
EXACT routed netlist. No synthesis, placement or routing change was allowed in
the white derivative. The user power-cycled before each load.

  original desktop: vertical dark lines (user report)
  constant white:   "White field is clean; lines are gone" (user report)
  original restored:"Desktop is back; the lines returned" (user report)

This is a repeatable data-dependent symptom. It does not prove a compositor
bug: constant white also removes RGB transitions, so a switching/output-path
problem remains possible. The result does not establish a hardware fix.

## Clock checks corrected, without changing the baseline hardware

The exact baseline's report tested these two clocks at nextpnr's default
12 MHz: keyboard_link.clk (the 50 MHz board clock AFTER its IBUF) and
desktop_sockets.pixel_clk (the 74.25 MHz CLKDIV output). The SDC only specified
sys_clk, clk and hclk5; constraining the input port's net did not constrain the
post-buffer net, and the pixel-clock alias had no constraint at all.

scripts/pnr-desktop.sh now invokes tools/desktop-clock-constraints.py to append
constraints at the PLL CLKIN/CLKOUT0 and CLKDIV CLKOUT pins. The helper refuses
a changed PLL/divider recipe rather than silently applying old frequencies.
The complete desktop build's timing gate now requires both domains at their
real frequencies, plus the main clock at its existing 21.49 MHz constraint.
tools/pnr-timing.py rejects a missing/wrong-frequency required domain even
when the log's own verdict says PASS.

Rerouting the unchanged source with PLL_R[0], seed 23 and corrected constraints:

  clock                       modeled maximum   checked at
  keyboard_link.clk           129.82 MHz        50.00 MHz
  desktop_sockets.pixel_clk   115.83 MHz        74.25 MHz
  clk                         74.97 MHz         21.49 MHz
  setup violations            0
  hold violations             0

Compared with the baseline JSON: identical cell sets, zero connection changes,
zero parameter changes, zero BEL changes and zero routing-string changes.
This corrects the verification gap; it does not change the FPGA's behavior or
explain the stripes. The acceptance result is limited to nextpnr's model.

nextpnr's SDC parser in this fork supports create_clock and set_false_path,
not set_output_delay. We did not invent a VGA output delay or claim that
register-to-register timing checks establish the analog waveform's quality.
The JSON critical-path report contains clocked paths, not a VGA pin report.

The existing TinyTang tools/tests/test_desktop_video.sh was run against the
actual reconstructed source used by this build. It passed all 1,237,500 VGA
raster pixels and 921,600 visible HDMI pixels against its independent reference,
including varying characters and foreground/background colours in every cell.
This checks the RTL under ideal simulated clocks, not FPGA routing or analog
output behavior. Clock-rate guard, changed-divider refusal, unrelated-clear
refusal, five colour overrides and per-log verdict checks also passed.

## Reproduction and remaining boundary

From this repository's root, with an existing reconstructed desktop tree:

  NEXTPNR_SEED=23 scripts/pnr-desktop.sh TREE
  tools/pnr-timing.py TREE/.open-pnr/nextpnr.log \
    --require-clock keyboard_link.clk=50 \
    --require-clock desktop_sockets.pixel_clk=74.25 \
    --require-clock clk=21.49
  tools/make-vga-solid.py TREE/.open-pnr/pnr.json white.json --rgb ffffff

Pack white.json with the fork's gowin_pack using the same environment as
scripts/build-desktop-core.sh, with NO *_as_gpio options, then convert its .fs
with tools/fs-to-bin.py. The manifest is the proof of unchanged routing.

tools/inspect-vga-routing.py is a nextpnr post-route hook, selected with
NEXTPNR_POST_ROUTE=/absolute/path/tools/inspect-vga-routing.py. It reports only
interconnect delay and combinational depth, excluding LUT propagation,
clock-to-Q and board delays. It follows RGB/raster registers, excluding clocked
OLED, socket-control and constant sources. The structural cone can include
other socket declarations/flips; these observations are not constraints or
device limits.

The hook completed on the corrected-clock build and recreated the baseline
connections, parameters, BELs and routing again. Fourteen data/sync pin cones
were reported. Longest structural interconnect-only paths from RGB/raster
registers range from 4.002 to 7.340 ns, through three combinational stages.
The longest cones reach the RGB MSB pins in row 108. These are structural
maxima over supported declarations/flips, not selected-mode propagation
measurements. In particular, some reported endpoints are reached from visible
or from another colour through an inactive mux alternative. Do not interpret
these numbers as measured analog settling or a demonstrated violation. The
RGB/blanking/personality path is combinational between the scanout registers
and the IOBUFs, and output waveform behavior still needs a changing-data test.
The individual pin records are kept in build/vga-diagnostic/vga-interconnect.txt.

Do not infer "pixel clock is too slow" from the old PASS-at-12 report: its
modeled maximum already exceeded 74.25 MHz, and corrected constraints recreated
the original routing. Do not infer that white being clean clears every dynamic
output path. The next discriminator is a known changing pixel pattern bypassing
the compositor, or a narrower isolation of the RGB register data and shared
clear. Keep the original image for comparisons and power-cycle before loads.
